The 24-Hour Economy was the flagship promise of President John Dramani Mahama's 2024 campaign, and it sits at the centre of the NDC government's plan to reset Ghana. The idea is simple: more businesses and public services working in shifts, around the clock, so that the same factories, markets and offices create more jobs.

## What the policy aims to do

In short, it is about **shifts, not shutdowns**. Instead of one working day, businesses that can operate longer are encouraged and supported to run two or three shifts. That means more hours of production and trade from the same buildings and equipment, and more people employed to run them.

For that to work, other things have to work too: security at night, reliable power, street lighting and transport. These are part of the conversation about how the policy is delivered.

## Why it matters here

Oforikrom already has a late-night economy. Think of:

- **Anloga**, where timber and furniture work keeps many hands busy and could keep more busy with longer hours.
- **Bomso, Ayeduase and Kotei**, where food sellers, printers, barbers and shops serve tens of thousands of students who study and live late.
- **Ayigya and Oforikrom town**, with markets and small businesses that would welcome safer, better-lit streets after dark.

Done well, more working hours here means more jobs for young people in the constituency, including graduates who want to stay in Kumasi.
